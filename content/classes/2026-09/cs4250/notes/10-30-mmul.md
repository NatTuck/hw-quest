The stereotypical parallel compute task is multiplying two matrices.

Unfortunately, we don't have linear algebra as a requirement in our CS program,
nor do we do any of the things that it enables, so why this is something you'd
want to do isn't immediately obvious, but here's the basic procedure:

- You can multiply an M×N matrix A with an N×P matrix B to get an M×P matrix C.
- Formula: `C[i][j] = sum(k = 1..n, A[i][k] * B[k][j])`
- That means each row of A multiplies with a column of B pairwise, and the sum
of that to is one cell of C.
- To make this a bit simpler, making N=M=P gives you that you can multiply
together two square matrices to get a square matrix of the same size.

Simple example cases:

- Identity matrix (diagonal 1's)
- Doubling matrix (diagonal 2's)
